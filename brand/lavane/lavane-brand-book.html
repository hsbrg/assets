The landscape

What the best vegetarian restaurants already know

We looked at eleven restaurants that shape how people think about vegetarian food, from a 1924 tiffin room in Bengaluru to a Michelin-starred dining room in Vienna. Some are direct competitors; others are lessons in how a food brand earns pride.

Each restaurant is placed by our own reading of two questions: whose table does it cook for, Indian or European, and is it an everyday place or a special occasion?

Where we stand

Lavané in one sentence

For people who care what is on their plate, Lavané is the pure vegetarian kitchen that cooks with European craft and Indian memory, where nothing is hidden, nothing is meat, and every meal begins with salt.

Guest one
“We have eaten pure veg all our lives. We just want somewhere beautiful to take the family.”

Lifelong vegetarian families who usually choose between trusted-but-plain and beautiful-but-uncertain. Lavané gives them both, and a promise they can rely on.

Guest two
“I'm not vegetarian. I just want a great dinner, not a lecture.”

Flexitarians and food lovers who come for flavour. Lavané never preaches. The pleasure of the food has to do the persuading.

Guest three
“I miss food that tastes like home, but cooked like this.”

Travellers and the Indian diaspora in global cities who want home flavours with the polish of a European dining room.

Lavané is
  • Proud to be vegetarian, and says so plainly
  • Generous, like a host who keeps coming back to the table
  • Exact about ingredients and where they come from
  • At home in Pune or Paris
Lavané is not
  • A health brand. We never count calories on the menu.
  • Preachy. We do not shame anyone's diet.
  • “Plant-based”. We say vegetarian, the word our guests use.
  • A fusion gimmick. Every dish has to stand on its own.

What we believe

Six house beliefs

लवणेन भोज्यम् lavaṇena bhojyam

“With salt, food becomes worth eating.” Salt is the oldest, simplest and most honest ingredient. It does not hide anything. It brings out what is already there. That is how we want to cook, and how we want to run the restaurant.

Salt first

Start with what is essential. A few honest ingredients, used well, beat a long list used to impress.

Every meal opens with a pinch of house salt, served at the table.

Never meat. Not once.

Our vegetarian promise is not a menu option or a trend. It is the reason the kitchen exists, from the first day to the last.

No meat, fish, egg or gelatin has ever entered the kitchen. Suppliers sign the same promise.

Nothing hidden

Clean food means you can see where it came from and how it was made.

An open kitchen window, and the farm's name next to every key ingredient on the menu.

Two tables, one meal

European craft and Indian memory are not opposites. A good risotto and a good khichdi want the same thing: patience and good rice.

Every season has one dish that crosses between the two tables.

Root to leaf

Respecting vegetables means using all of them. Waste is the opposite of clean.

Stems, peels and trimmings go into stocks, chutneys and pickles.

A fair kitchen is a clean kitchen

Purity includes the people who cook and serve. A meal cannot be clean if the people behind it are treated badly.

Fair pay, fair hours, and the team's names on the wall beside the farmers'.

The promise

What “pure vegetarian” means at Lavané

Pure veg is a promise of trust, so it has to be specific. This is the promise printed on every table, at every location.

Never servedMeat, poultry, fish, seafood and eggs.
Never hiddenGelatin, animal rennet, fish sauce, meat or fish stocks, and animal fats in frying.
Always declaredDairy and honey are marked on the menu. Every cheese is made with vegetarian rennet.
On requestJain and sattvic dishes, without onion, garlic or root vegetables, marked J on the menu.
CheckedSuppliers sign the promise. The ingredient list for every dish is available at the counter.
A decision for the founders: this book recommends no eggs at all. In India “pure veg” is widely understood to mean eggless, and an egg-free kitchen keeps the trust of Jain and many Hindu families. Abroad it costs little, since most guests will not miss egg on a menu designed without it.

How we speak

A host, never a preacher

Lavané talks like a good host: warm, precise and quietly proud. Whether a sentence is on a menu, a sign or a post, it should sound like it was said at the table.

Warm

not syrupy

We welcome people the way a family welcomes guests at home.

Precise

not technical

We name the farm, the season and the spice. We don't use jargon.

Proud

not preachy

We are proud to be vegetarian and never shame other diets.

Plain

not fancy

Short sentences. One idea at a time. French or Sanskrit only where it adds meaning.

The identity

One leaf, one grain of salt, two scripts

From the earlier explorations, the system settles on the Soft Leaf: the crystal leaf drawn by hand, with a single grain of saffron salt. It is simple enough to work as a 16-pixel icon and warm enough to carve into wood.

Primary logo

Leaf, wordmark, tagline and verse. Use it on signage, menus and anywhere with room to breathe.

Primary, reversed

Sea salt and sage on basil. For the fascia, uniforms and dark packaging.

Horizontal

For website headers, receipts and narrow spaces.

Devanagari

लवणे with शुद्ध शाकाहारी भोजनालय. Use it alongside the primary logo on Indian signage, never instead of it.

Verse seal

For wax seals, the bill folder and stamps.

House emblem

For brass, engraving and special editions only.

The leaf

The mark on its own: app icon, favicon, social avatar and embossing.

Small-size test

The leaf stays recognisable down to favicon size. Below 24 px, use the one-colour version without the salt grain.

Rituals

Things guests will tell their friends about

Logos get noticed. Rituals get retold. These six small habits turn the brand's beliefs into something guests can see and feel.

On arrival

The first pinch

A small brass bowl of house salt arrives before anything else, with the words “Salt first”. It echoes the banana-leaf meal, where salt is served first, at the top left.

At the end

The verse on the bill

The bill folder carries लवणेन भोज्यम् with its meaning. Guests leave with the story, not just the receipt.

Always

The kitchen window

An open window into the kitchen. Anyone can watch their food being cooked. This is our most convincing proof that the food is clean.

Every week

The growers' board

A chalkboard names this week's farms, along with the cooks on shift. Farmers and staff are credited side by side.

Every season

The crossing dish

One dish each season crosses between the two tables, for example saffron risotto with curry-leaf butter. It becomes a reason to come back.

On every table

The promise card

The Lavané Promise sits on every table in two scripts, so no guest ever has to ask.
