Logo design · Top pick: Leaf-Crystal

One grain of salt, one leaf. That is the whole idea.

Lavane is a pure vegetarian restaurant that takes its name from lavaṇa, the Sanskrit word for salt. The mark joins the two things the brand stands for: the honest essential ingredient, and a kitchen that never serves meat. The wordmark is set in Roman inscriptional capitals, so it reads as a European dining room anywhere in the world.

Design language

Native at the root, European on the plate

Every logo decision traces back to one of three brand truths. If a shape could not be explained by one of them, it was cut.

From lavaṇa, salt

Faceted, crystalline cuts

Salt is a crystal. The symbol is drawn only with straight facets and clean cut-lines, like a mineral. It signals purity and honesty without saying “organic”.

From pure vegetarian

A leaf, never a garnish

The crystal's silhouette is a leaf, its cut-lines are veins, and it ends in a stem. Vegetarian is the brand's only distinction, so it lives inside the symbol rather than in a badge.

From a global table

Roman capitals, wide tracking

“Lavane” already sounds French or Italian. Flared Roman capitals lean into that, so the name sits easily beside a bistro in Paris or a dining room in Pune. Devanagari appears only as a quiet secondary signature.

Concepts

Five routes, one recommendation

Each route tests a different way of telling the same story. All five share the palette and wordmark, so any of them can be developed further.

Top pick

1 · Leaf-Crystal

Symbol + wordmark

A salt crystal cut into the shape of a leaf. The facets are the veins, and the stem anchors it as something grown. The dark left half and lighter right half give it a gem-like turn of light.

Why it wins: it is the only route that says salt and vegetarian in a single shape. It works as a one-colour stamp, an app icon and an embossed seal, and it does not depend on the tagline to be understood.

2 · Salt Grain

Isometric crystal

A single cubic salt grain, shaded on three faces. Very clean and modern.

Trade-off: it says “salt” clearly but not “vegetarian”, and a cube can read as tech or logistics.

3 · Arch & Sprout

Doorway emblem

A Roman arch that rises into a soft Indian point, with a sprout and a saffron sun inside. It reads as a doorway into the restaurant.

Trade-off: the warmest story, but the most illustrative, and it loses detail at small sizes.

4 · V-Sprout

Wordmark only

The V in Lavane is made of two leaves, so the name carries the vegetarian promise by itself.

Trade-off: elegant on signage, but no standalone symbol for icons and stamps. Worth keeping as an alternate wordmark.

5 · Seal

Badge with लवण

A round seal holding the Leaf-Crystal, with लवण (lavaṇa) set in Devanagari. This is where the native root becomes visible.

Trade-off: too detailed for a primary logo. It works best as a secondary mark on packaging, wax seals and the bill folder.

Next steps

Taking the Leaf-Crystal forward

  1. Confirm the verse. Share the exact Sanskrit line and its source so the Devanagari on the seal and menus is correct.
  2. Refine the symbol. Fine-tune facet angles and the stem curve at 16px, 64px and signage size, and make a heavier version for small print.
  3. Customise the wordmark. Marcellus is the base. A designer should redraw the A crossbars and the N to make it fully ownable before trademark filing.
  4. Check the name. Run trademark and domain searches for “Lavane” in your launch markets.
